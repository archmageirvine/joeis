Skelet #17: soundness of the Java certificate
==============================================

SkeletSeventeen checks the actual transition table, nine local tape
identities, and the blank-tape initialization. DyadicProof.Ladder
checks the algebra below universally, not at a cutoff width. The
generic induction rules linking these checks are given here. This is
a specialized Java verifier, not a port of the Rocq proof kernel.

1. What a counter transition means
----------------------------------

Write P=01 for a pair on the near side of the left stack and Q=10 for
a pair on the right. A list (a0,...,aw) means C looking left into
P^a0 1 P^a1 1 ... 1 P^aw followed by blanks, with blank right stack.
The base replay reaches (0,4,2,0) from the all-blank initial machine.
These are nearest-first counters, the reverse order from Xu's paper.

The raw checker follows each rule to its exact target without reading
an opaque tail. Thus the rule holds for every substitution for its
tails. The one-pair B sweep and the two-pair C sweep extend to all
right runs and all even left runs respectively, by induction on the
number of applications. An even group followed by a separator moves
onto the right stack as 1 Q^a. When C reaches an odd nonterminal
group, sweep its even part, then use the checked 011 turn. Sweeping
back over the even part leaves P^(a-1) 1 P^(b+1) in front of the next
tail: the odd group a has lost one and its next group b gained one.

On the return, the checked 110 rule plus right sweeps change a right
group 1 Q^t (t>0) into a left prefix P^(t-1) 1 P. This puts t-1 at
the head and adds one to its old near counter, so successive positive
groups restore all internal counts. The final positive a0 becomes
a0-1; the rule on blank stops at C. If the last group is 1 followed by
blank (a0=0), the checked empty-group rule supplies one P to the
previously decremented counter and drops that empty group. We describe
this as decrement to a0=-1 followed by Halve, which drops a0. The
negative intermediate is bookkeeping, not a physical configuration.

At a terminal even y the checked blank-left turn gives 11 then blank
on the left. Sweeping back Q^y gives the list (y,0,0) before restoring
the skipped groups; the restored separator increments y to y+1.
This is Zero: increment the old top, append two zeros, subtract one
from a0 and normalize a0=-1 by Halve. At a terminal odd y, the odd
blank-left turn gives (1,1,0,0), then returning over y-1 pairs gives
(y,1,0,0) before restoring the skipped groups. With a0=0, the resulting
raw list is (a1,...,y+1,1,0,0). We represent this as Overflow to the
virtual list (0,a1,...,y+1,0), followed by its Zero and immediate
Halve. Only the combined path is claimed on the physical tape.

2. Gray counter and return to zero
---------------------------------

At width w, define the Gray bits gi = ai mod 2 (1 <= i <= w).
Binary bit i-1 is the XOR of gi through gw. Let n be that binary
integer, and let the direction be up iff the sum of all ai is odd.
The direction is the low binary bit XOR the parity of a0. When a0
is odd the next increment flips the first Gray bit. Otherwise the
first odd higher group is at the far edge of the initial run of
equal low binary bits. Adding one to a run of ones or subtracting
one from a run of zeros changes just the next Gray bit. This proves
the +/-1 rule. Each increment takes one unit from a0 and adds one
at the changed Gray position, preserving the direction. If a0=-1,
Halve drops it, halves n and reverses the direction.

Call a list regular when a1,...,a_(w-1) are positive and the last
counter is arbitrary. Interior steps preserve regularity, since
they only increase the higher counters. Zero from n=0 descending is
legal on a regular list: all counters are then even. It produces two
high zero counters, all other higher counters positive, and n=2^w-1
descending. In that mode the current effective width excludes the two
high zero counters. A descending run stopping at n>=3 cannot inspect
the blank high boundary, and halving then leaves n>=1. An ascending
run crossing the effective upper bound hits the highest positive
counter as its odd group and fills the first high zero; it becomes
regular, with just one high zero. These facts justify the changes
of mode used below, without assuming that arbitrary Zero is safe on
a list already ending in two zeros.

Let w be odd, A=2^(w-1), and E a regular even (empty) list. The
three monotone runs of its two-halving return N have endpoints:

    after Zero:            n=2A-1, descending
    first run then Halve:  s1=2A-1-a0, h1=(s1-1)/2
    second run then Halve: s2=A+a1,    h2=s2/2
    third run:             h2 down to 0

The first two runs end exactly when the low counter becomes -1.
The checked guards 0<=a0<=2A-4, 2<=a1<=3A-2, both even, give
s1>=3 and A+2<=s2<=4A-2: the upward run crosses the effective-width
boundary, but cannot overflow. The low counter just after the first
Halve is a1+a0/2, giving the stated s2. The third run is legal
provided its final a0 is nonnegative. These rules imply regularity
at the end of the return and a positive number of real steps.

Put r_i(t)=floor((t+2^(i-1))/2^i), i>=1. Along consecutive binary
values, Gray bit i flips exactly at the points counted by the
change of r_i. The number of increments at that coordinate along
a monotone run is therefore the absolute difference in r_i at its
endpoints. Applying this to the three runs gives N's low counter
a2+A/2-2r_1(h1). At j>=1 it gives

    shifted post-Zero counter at j+2
       + r_(j+2)(2A-1) - 2r_(j+1)(h1) + 2r_j(h2).

Here r_(j+2)(2h1+1)=r_(j+1)(h1) and r_(j+1)(2h2)=r_j(h2)
follow by integer division; the first shifted counter includes the
increment of the old top if j+2=w, and zero fill above w.

These formulas prove the carry rule used to propagate returns.
With coordinates in range, adding 2 at i>=2 shifts the perturbation
to i-2. At i=0 it lowers h1 by one and sends the 2 to index v2(h1).
At i=1 it raises h2 by one and sends the 2 to v2(h2+1)+1. Indeed
r_j(t)-r_j(t-1) is 1 exactly when t is 2^(j-1) times an odd integer.
The low-coordinate formula gives the same rule at index zero.
The perturbed output is therefore still nonnegative if the old
return was valid, once the input bounds of the new return hold.
Induction gives the descending chain i,i-2,...,i mod 2. The last
index is its root, and its predecessor h's are those before the
final low-coordinate change. This is the only carry induction used.

3. A width increases by two
--------------------------

Let S_w be (0,2^(w-1),2^(w-2),...,2,0), w odd >=3. The symbolic
checker verifies N(S_w)=S_w with 2 added at w. The first chain visits
w,w-2,...,1. The following four chains, with valuations checked
modulo 4, visit [1], [2,0], [0], and [1]. This starts the middle
phase at checkpoint m=2, root 1, predecessor (h1,h2)=(A-3,A+2).
The low counters are (2m,A+2m+2).

For a checkpoint m of odd 2-adic valuation, take the next m'>m
with odd valuation, ending at A-2 (which has valuation one). During
the first half, for e=m+1,...,m', the current root 1 selects chain
v2(A+e)+1. This is v2(e)+1: for 0<e<A, write e=2^v*t, t odd,
and A/2^v is even. Each intermediate e has even valuation, hence
the chain ends at 1; at m' it ends at 0. During the second half,
root 0 selects v2(A-e)=v2(e) over the same range. Intermediates
end at 0 and the last at 1. The predecessor h's decrease h1 once
per low-zero addition and increase h2 once per low-one addition,
giving (A-m'-1,A+m') at the next checkpoint. For each e the pair
of chains covers positions 0 through v2(e)+1 exactly once: the
parities alternate and the sole higher endpoint is v2(e)+1.
All low coordinates are monotone, and the checked terminal guard
therefore bounds every preceding return. The A=4 case has no
middle iterations.

At coordinate j, the middle additions are the number of multiples
of 2^max(0,j-1) in [3,A-2]. Java checks that floor difference, adds
the prefix and the final low-one chain, and verifies the resulting
vector. The final low-one addition leaves a0=2A-4 and a1=3A.
There is now a different return: Zero and a descending run reach
n=3 with low counter -1, Halve yields n=1, and the ascending run
reaches n=4A-1 with low counter zero. These endpoint and per-index
identities, including positivity and the highest-only odd parity,
are checked symbolically. The resulting Overflow is fused with
the next Zero as in part 1. The virtual regular state has width
w+2, low pair (0,4A-4), and h's (4A-1,4A-2).

Java verifies that the first two-halving return from that virtual
state adds 2 at index w, and all other coordinates are unchanged.
The corresponding odd chain adds 2 down through index 1. It is
safe by the checked low guards, including after the last addition.
The predecessor h2 for the last low addition is 4A-2, so the next
carry is v2(4A-1)+1=1. A final addition at 1 completes S_(w+2).
The whole path consumes positive real steps. Starting at S_3, this
proves endless progress and hence excludes a halting step.

What the symbolic checker covers
--------------------------------

Each cone uses two independent nonnegative integer parameters. The
cases are w=3 explicitly, and w>=5 split into j=0,1,2, the two
parities of 3<=j<=w-3, and j=w-2,w-1,w,w+1,w+2. They are an exact
cover. Powers of two with affine exponents are added exactly with
BigInteger. Rounding separates integer terms and bounds remaining
dyadic fractions, and fails unless both bounds force the same
integer. Bounds and residues are proved on the cone: testing a few
values of the parameters cannot pass the symbolic checker. The
separate skelet17-control mode is only a finite regression.

Sources for the counter structure and induction: Chris Xu,
https://arxiv.org/html/2407.02426v2, and the pinned Coq-BB5 v1.0.0
CoqBB5/BB5/BB5_Skelet17.v. The finite tape schemas and normalized
dyadic identities are replayed by Java, without assuming either
source's final nonhalting theorem as a verdict.
